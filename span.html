FULL CASE STUDY
01 / START
OVERVIEW

SPAN is a fictional structural engineering firm built to prove I could carry one idea through a complete pipeline: research, wireframes, brand identity, high-fidelity design, and a fully responsive, coded build. I chose civil engineering deliberately — my research kept pulling toward restrained, trust-building visuals, and I'm genuinely drawn to that raw-steel, job-site aesthetic.

02 / STUDY
RESEARCH

I collected reference sites and annotated real patterns — scroll fades, tactile hovers, one-accent-color palettes, sticky shrink-on-scroll navs. I also set a hard rule early: no lag. Several of my favorite references were stunning but heavy — restraint became a deliberate feature of the brand, not a compromise.

03 / IDENTITY
BRAND & LOGO

The logo went through real iteration — my first attempt at building a bridge arch into the "A" drifted into looking like a highway interchange. Testing it at favicon size is what exposed the problem, and rebuilding it with symmetric pen-tool anchors fixed it for good.

04 / BUILD
DEVELOPMENT

Built from scratch in vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no framework. The trickiest bug: a mobile nav menu that toggled correctly but stayed invisible, which turned out to be a z-index stacking conflict with the hero's own layered pseudo-elements, not a JavaScript problem at all.

05 / REVIEW
REFLECTION

The real lesson was separating research from decoration — knowing which patterns served the content versus which just looked cool. I also came out of this with a debugging habit I didn't have going in: check the browser Console first, before assuming my own logic is wrong.
